# SONIC editorial proposal

## Start with the edit the sound has to fit

A handled sound effect has a job. An interface click has to finish before the next input. A machine loop has to repeat without giving away the edit. A rain bed has to sit under a voice. A transition has to land on a cut. I would organize the first four hands around those jobs. A player should be able to take the same hand to a different source and recognize the same editing decision in the prompt.

The current deck has **12 starting hands, 38 lineages and 114 graded faces**. The starting hands use 34 distinct lineages. Stutter, Decay Tail, Gritty and Forceful are available in the picker but absent from the presets. Of these, Decay Tail addresses a conspicuous omission. Many hands tell us how the sound starts. Few tell us when the editor gets silence back.

This is a review of the shipped text, not a listening test. The examples below use the opening card terms in composer order. No audio was requested or auditioned.

**Add two opening hands after the pilot.** **Foley Contact** would start with a gesture, contact surface and listener distance. Two passes of cloth over wood should stay two passes. **Room Tone** would hold a location steady between dialogue edits, with a controllable noise floor and no foreground event. Machine Loop owns a repeating mechanism; Room Tone owns what is left when the foreground stops. Keep the original IDs and saved selections accessible during any migration.

## Four new controls worth piloting

These grades illustrate one change at a time. Keep source identity, event count, timing supplied in the brief and listening position stable unless the player selects the card that changes them.

| Card | First grade | Middle grade | Strongest grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Release length** | Damp the sound just after the attack. | Let the source ring briefly, then settle. | Leave a clearly audible decay within the requested time window. |
| **Bed consistency** | Keep the named texture present throughout. | Keep its level and density steady under foreground speech. | Suppress isolated accents and conspicuous rises across the entire bed. |
| **Loop seam** | Match the timbre at the start and end. | Carry level, speed and tone across the join. | Also align recurring pulses across the join; keep starts, stops and one-off events in separate layers. |
| **Arrival point** | Name where the motion peaks relative to the cut. | Align the peak with the requested edit point. | Shape the lead-in and release around that same point, preserving any silence requested after it. |

**Mixing controls should be optional.** A listener-position card helps when a door is heard from the next room. It gets in the way when every interface beep inherits a small room. For a later edition I would add **Surface contact** and **Speech intelligibility** after checking whether existing material and distance cards can already express the difference.

## Four-hand text pilot

| Hand | First brief and current opening | Proposed prompt | Second brief and pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interface Click | A felt-covered switch confirms a selection and ends dry. The current hand adds “subtle synthetic tone, delicate glassy tone” and “in a small room.” | “Felt-covered switch closing, one close muted tick, short dry release, silence before the next input.” | A glass touch key with a brief ringing confirmation. Keep one event and let it ring. The reusable hand must lose the felt and the dry cutoff. |
| Rain Bed | Light roof rain beneath dialogue, heard from inside. The current opening exports “light drizzle, damp.” Its Outdoors card falls beyond the ambience cap. | “Light rain on a roof heard indoors, soft even patter, steady beneath dialogue.” | Fine rain outside on a canvas awning. Keep the canvas texture and outdoor position. No indoor filtering or foreground drops. |
| Machine Loop | A desk fan runs steadily. The current hand adds “machine whirring, metallic, steady seamless loop, very loud, tight room acoustics.” | “Desk fan at steady speed, quiet blade flutter, level and tone continuous through the loop seam.” | A projector with a regular mechanical pulse. Preserve the pulse through the join. No fan blades, startup, shutdown or new speed. |
| Whoosh Transition | A paper page sweeps into a cut, followed immediately by silence. The current hand adds “fast whoosh, rising sweep, bright, crisp, cinematic.” | “Paper page sweeping past, dry flutter building to the cut, peak on the cut, immediate silence.” | A slow fabric pass that peaks halfway and trails away. Preserve the new halfway peak and soft tail, with no paper flutter or abrupt cutoff. |

The proposed words specify what a future listener should check. They make no claim about the audio a renderer would produce. Pilot with text first, remove one defining card at a time, and mark where the instruction actually disappears. For a later approved listening test, replay a loop three times, place a bed under the same spoken line, and audition transitions against the same edit. Compare at matched playback level except when level itself is the variable.

## What the composer and exports need

- **Keep the one-line prompt and separate layer rack.** The existing composer cleans the sound line, removes identical fragments, and writes one prompt per sound. A glass hit and a settling-debris layer can then be placed separately. Keep layer-specific facts out of the next hand's skill.
- **Make ambience selection visible.** Ambience mode currently retains the first two card fragments in merge order. Rain Bed loses Outdoors; Crowd Murmur keeps “distant chatter, slightly distant” and loses Small Room. Preserve the short prompt and let the player choose the two descriptors that matter. Show an equipped card when it is omitted from the export.
- **Keep the five boards and one Emphasis dial for the first pilot.** Onset currently has one slot. Put release in an independent ending control or give onset and release separate positions within the same board. A hand must be able to specify both a sharp attack and a long tail.
- **One-off prompt:** take the current source, timing, location and edit point from the brief. **Reusable skill:** retain the job of the hand. Machine Loop should ask for the new mechanism and cycle, then match the seam. It should carry neither the fan nor its speed into the projector brief.
- **First decision:** test the four refits on these eight briefs before hiding any old cards, widening the rack or commissioning audio or art.
